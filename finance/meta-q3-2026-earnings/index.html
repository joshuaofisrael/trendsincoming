The quick version

Meta grew fast and spent faster. Revenue rose 26% to $64.60 billion in the third quarter of 2026, but the company lifted its 2026 capital spending plan to $135 to $145 billion, posted negative free cash flow and announced a new nonvoting Class C stock. Shares fell 7.84% to $648.85 on Thursday, October 22, per Sharecast.

Why the stock fell

Investors liked the growth but balked at the bill. CNBC highlighted that free cash flow turned negative as AI data center spending climbed, and Reuters reported investors weighing growth in Meta's Muse AI against rising costs. Meta's drop helped push the Nasdaq to its worst day since June on Thursday.

What's the Class C stock?

Meta said it is creating a new class of nonvoting shares. In plain terms, they're shares that carry economic ownership without a say in shareholder votes. Details on how and when they'll be used are in Meta's release, so read the source before acting on headlines.
