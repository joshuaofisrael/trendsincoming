Boeing 737 MAX 10

Oct 20✈️ Travel

Boeing 737 MAX 10 is certified

The FAA certified the largest 737 MAX on Oct 20 after years of delays. It seats up to 230, adds an enhanced angle-of-attack system and was the first jet certified under the full 2020 safety law. WestJet is the launch customer, with service expected in early 2027.

The quick version

The Federal Aviation Administration certified the Boeing 737 MAX 10 on Tuesday, October 20, 2026. It's the biggest member of the 737 MAX family, and approval lets Boeing build and deliver it to airlines in the U.S. and abroad. Boeing says deliveries begin in the coming months, with Canada's WestJet as launch customer and service expected in early 2027.

What changes for flyers

Not much yet. The first MAX 10s won't carry passengers until 2027, starting with WestJet. United has said it expects its first MAX 10 in mid to late 2027, and plans to use it on major routes with more premium seats than the jets it replaces. If you want to check what plane you're on, your airline's booking page or app lists the aircraft type for each flight.

Why it took so long

The MAX 10 was first expected around 2020. Certification slowed after the 737 MAX crisis, then over an engine anti-ice issue, and most recently a software glitch in late September that paused the process. The FAA later found that glitch was not a safety concern. Before signing off, the FAA required updates to the flight-control system, including the enhanced angle-of-attack sensor, in line with the 2020 safety law and NTSB recommendations.
