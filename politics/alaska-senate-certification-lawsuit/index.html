Alaska Senate certification lawsuit

By The Trends Incoming team

The Justice Department has sued Alaska election officials to block certification of the Nov 3 U.S. Senate result in one scenario: if Sen. Dan Sullivan loses and votes for a second candidate named Daniel J. Sullivan could have changed the outcome. What the suit asks for, the backstory and what happens next, explained neutrally.

What's trending in Alaska Senate certification lawsuit

The quick version

The U.S. Justice Department has filed a federal lawsuit asking a court to stop Alaska from certifying its Nov 3 U.S. Senate result in one specific case: if incumbent Republican Sen. Dan S. Sullivan loses, and the votes cast for a second candidate, Daniel J. Sullivan, would have been enough to change the outcome under Alaska's ranked choice count. The suit was filed late Thursday, October 22, AP reports. The race pits Sen. Sullivan against Democrat Mary Peltola.

What the lawsuit argues

The complaint argues that the second Sullivan's candidacy could confuse voters who mean to back the senator, and that Alaska should not certify a result that may be affected by that confusion. The Justice Department's filing quotes the June 15 finding by the Alaska Division of Elections, which said the declaration of candidacy was filed to confuse or mislead voters.

The other side of the story

Alaska's own courts ruled that Daniel J. Sullivan could stay on the ballot after the Division of Elections tried to remove him, NBC News reports, and the ballot labels the incumbent as the registered Republican and incumbent. AP notes that many fear the case could become a lengthy fight between the federal government and states over certifying elections. President Trump told reporters on Friday, "I'm the one that said do it," per AP.

What happens next

A federal judge will decide whether the case moves forward and whether to grant any order. Voting continues as normal, and Election Day is Tuesday, November 3. No ruling had been reported when we checked on October 24. We will update this page with court filings as they come in, and we link the full complaint below so you can read it yourself.

Quick answers

Why is the Justice Department suing Alaska?

It argues that a second Senate candidate named Daniel J. Sullivan could mislead voters who intend to vote for Sen. Dan S. Sullivan, and it wants certification blocked if those votes could have changed the result.

Does the lawsuit stop people from voting in Alaska?

No. It targets certification after the election, not voting. Election Day is November 3, 2026.

Who is running for Senate in Alaska in 2026?

The main contenders are Republican Sen. Dan S. Sullivan and Democrat Mary Peltola. Daniel J. Sullivan is also on the ballot.

Why is Daniel J. Sullivan still on the ballot?

Alaska election officials removed him in June, finding his candidacy was not in good faith, but Alaska courts ruled he could stay on, NBC News reports.

Has a judge ruled on the Alaska certification lawsuit?

No ruling had been reported as of October 24, 2026. The suit was filed late on October 22.
