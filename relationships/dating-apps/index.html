The quick version

The dating app business is being reshuffled. Bumble agreed on October 12, 2026 to be acquired by Ithaca Holdings in an all-cash deal valuing it at $1.3 billion, or $6.05 a share, a 138% premium to its October 9 close, per its SEC filing. Meanwhile, Hinge keeps gaining paying users while Tinder's shrink.

What the Bumble deal means for users

Nothing changes overnight. Bumble says it will run as a private company with offices in London, Austin and New York, and Ithaca's co-CEOs Ravi Gupta and John Hegeman will become co-CEOs of Bumble after closing. Wolfe Herd said "the best is yet to come" in the joint release.

The bigger trend

Singles are tired of endless swiping. Reviewers point to a shift toward apps built for real dates and verified profiles, which helps explain Hinge's growth.
