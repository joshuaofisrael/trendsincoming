Social Security COLA 2027 is official: 3.6%

By The Trends Incoming team · Published 2026-10-14

Heads up: This page is for informational and entertainment purposes only. It is not financial, investment or tax advice, and it is not a recommendation to buy or sell any security. Data may be delayed or inaccurate. Do your own research or consult a licensed financial advisor. Trends Incoming and its writers may hold positions in securities mentioned.

It's official. The Social Security Administration says Social Security and SSI benefits rise 3.6% in 2027. That's the number on ssa.gov/cola, not a forecast screenshot. About 76 million Americans are covered by the bump.

Planning beyond Social Security? See the 2027 tax brackets and standard deduction in our 2027 tax numbers tracker.

What a COLA is

Every year, Social Security and SSI benefits get adjusted so they keep up with inflation. It isn't a raise Congress votes on. The formula is set by law. SSA compares the average of a specific inflation index, the CPI-W, for July, August and September with the same three months a year earlier. The percentage change, rounded to a tenth of a point, becomes the COLA. If prices fall, benefits stay put rather than going down.

The official 2027 numbers

  • COLA: 3.6% (up from 2.8% for 2026).
  • Social Security: higher payments for nearly 72 million beneficiaries begin with benefits payable in January 2027.
  • SSI: about 7.3 million recipients see the increase starting December 31, 2026.
  • Taxable maximum: earnings subject to Social Security tax rise to $192,300.
  • Earnings limit (under full retirement age all year): $25,560 ($1 deducted for each $2 over).
  • Earnings limit (year you reach full retirement age): $67,920 until the month before you hit full retirement age ($1 for each $3 over).
  • No earnings limit once you're full retirement age for the entire year.

Why it's trending

Because the wait is over. Ahead of the announcement, forecasters were clustered around 3.5% to 3.6%. AARP and others had been quoted in that range; The Senior Citizens League had also floated 3.5%. The official figure landed at the high end of that band, and it's the biggest COLA in three years. People are doing the math on their January checks, and social feeds lit up the second SSA posted it.

There's still a flip side. Medicare Part B premiums, which are often deducted from Social Security checks, also tend to rise. That can eat part of the raise. Medicare's 2027 numbers come later; SSA points people to medicare.gov for those details.

The backstory (forecasts as context)

COLA announcements usually land in mid-October with the September CPI report. Last year a government shutdown delayed the data. This year the schedule held. Forecasts drifted all year: TSCL was as high as 3.9% in the spring, then 3.6%, then about 3.5% as monthly inflation data came in. Those estimates were useful for planning. They're history now. The official source is SSA.

When you'll actually see it

  • The COLA applies to December 2026 benefits, paid in January 2027.
  • SSI's first adjusted payment lands December 31, 2026.
  • COLA notices show up in late November in many my Social Security Message Centers (create or sign in by November 18, 2026 if you want the online notice).
  • There's nothing to apply for. The adjustment is automatic.

What's next

Medicare open enrollment runs October 15 through December 7. That's the window to compare 2027 Medicare Advantage and Part D plans before the COLA and premium changes hit. (HealthCare.gov's separate open enrollment opens November 1.) See our Medicare open enrollment hub and the retirement & Social Security hub.

Quick answers

When will I see the increase?

Social Security benefits payable in January 2027. SSI increases begin December 31, 2026.

Do I need to apply?

No. The COLA is automatic.

What is the 2027 taxable maximum?

$192,300, per SSA.
