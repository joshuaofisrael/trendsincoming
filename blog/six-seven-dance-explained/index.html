The "Six Seven" dance: why your whole feed is counting on its fingers

By The Trends Incoming team · 2026-10-15

If you've opened TikTok this month, you've seen it: someone counts on their fingers, then shrugs right on the beat. That's the "Six Seven" dance, and Lightreel's October trend report calls it the platform's dominant trend right now.

What it is

The sound is a Brazilian funk track built around, of all things, a math problem. The vocalist counts through an addition problem in Portuguese. Dancers count along on their fingers, then everyone hits the same shrug on the drop. That's basically it. Simple, silly, and weirdly satisfying.

Why it's trending

Two words: J-Hope. When the BTS member joined in, the trend exploded. According to Lightreel, the biggest single Six Seven video has passed 75 million views, and fan compilations of his version became their own mini-trend. GenZ NewZ reported one compilation pulling nearly three million plays.

The bigger reason is that it's legible. You understand the whole joke in two seconds. You don't need to speak Portuguese, because the counting is right there on the dancers' hands. You don't need dance skills either. That's why it jumped from Brazilian dance floors to K-pop fandom to people who swear they never post dances.

The backstory

Viral dances usually need a spark. Six Seven had a catchy, easy-to-copy hook, and then got the biggest spark possible: a global superstar doing it. It also fits a pattern Lightreel spotted across TikTok this month. There's no single universal challenge. Instead, lots of "remixable micro-formats" are all accelerating at once: Halloween costume transitions, K-pop revivals, cat edits and more. Six Seven just happens to be the loudest one.

How to do it (30-second version)

  1. Find the sound on TikTok (search "six seven" or tap the hashtag link below).
  2. Count along on your fingers with the lyric.
  3. Shrug, big and on beat, when it drops.
  4. Bonus points: add a caption that makes it specific to you. That's what's working best this month.

What's next

Trends like this tend to peak and then mutate. Expect costume versions as Halloween gets closer, plus pet versions, group versions and inevitably brand versions. If you want in, sooner is better. Once the brands pile on, the cool kids move on.

Where to follow it

The hashtag links below take you straight to the trend on TikTok, Instagram, YouTube and more. For the "why" behind it, Lightreel and NewEngen publish solid weekly trend reports.

Quick answers

What is the Six Seven dance?

A TikTok trend set to a Brazilian funk track. You count along on your fingers with a Portuguese addition problem, then shrug on the beat.

Who made it go viral?

BTS member J-Hope's version pushed it into the mainstream. Lightreel says the biggest single video has passed 75 million views.

Do I need to know Portuguese?

Nope. The counting is visible on your hands, which is a big part of why it travels so well.

Is it still worth posting?

Trends move fast, but as of mid-October it's still the top format according to trend reports. Add a personal twist in your caption to stand out.
