Hormuz, the Houthis and why a strip of water matters to your gas tank

By The Trends Incoming team · 2026-10-15

Nonpartisan explainer: sourced facts, both sides represented, no endorsements.

Two stories out of the Middle East are tangled together right now: a sharp escalation between Saudi Arabia and Yemen's Houthi movement, and rising tension around the Strait of Hormuz. Here's a calm, sourced rundown. Lives are being lost, so we're skipping the hype and sticking to what wire services and established outlets have reported.

What's happening

The Saudi–Houthi escalation: Al Jazeera and PBS report that a Houthi attack on Riyadh's international airport over the weekend killed at least 13 people and injured more than 300. Another person was killed in a second strike on Sunday. On October 13, the Houthis targeted an Aramco administrative building in Dhahran, according to Saudi officials. The Saudi-led coalition launched airstrikes on oil facilities in Houthi-held Yemen, including in Sanaa. Several international airlines, including Delta, Lufthansa, Air France-KLM and Qatar Airways, suspended flights to Saudi Arabia and neighboring countries.

Hormuz: AP reports that the Institute for the Study of War said Iran appears to be targeting commercial vessels doing ship-to-ship oil transfers in and near the Strait of Hormuz, in an effort to undermine the movement of energy through the strait.

Why it's trending

Because geography matters. The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow passage that a huge share of the world's oil tankers move through. On the other side of the Arabian Peninsula, the Bab al-Mandab strait is the southern gateway to the Red Sea, and PBS notes the Houthis have targeted shipping there. When both chokepoints are tense at once, energy traders get nervous, and people start asking about gas prices.

You could see the nerves in US markets. On October 13, refiners like Valero, Phillips 66 and Marathon Petroleum rose about 4% to 5%, with US diesel futures up roughly 5% by midday, according to a market recap citing NBC News.

The backstory

Saudi Arabia has backed Yemen's government against the Houthis for years. Al Jazeera reports that Saudi Arabia recently launched strikes and is backing a Yemeni government counteroffensive after the Houthis seized strategic territory last month. PBS also reports that Turkey's president plans to send a Saudi–Turkey–Pakistan defense pact to parliament. Meanwhile, Saudi Arabia is pushing Washington for more direct military support, and the US has so far stopped short of sending military assets to attack Yemen.

What's next

  • Watch for de-escalation efforts. The International Committee of the Red Cross has called on all parties to de-escalate, warning that civilians are bearing the brunt.
  • Watch airline announcements if you're traveling to the Gulf region.
  • Watch energy prices. Sustained disruption to shipping would show up there first.
