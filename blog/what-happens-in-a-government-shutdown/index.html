What happens in a government shutdown? Social Security, mail, federal pay

By The Trends Incoming team · Published 2026-11-09

Nonpartisan explainer: sourced facts, both sides represented, no endorsements.

Short answer: A government shutdown happens when Congress has not passed funding, either full-year appropriations or a short-term continuing resolution, by the deadline. Agencies then have to stop work paid for by annual appropriations unless it is excepted by law, and non-excepted employees are furloughed. Social Security and SSI payments continue on their normal dates, mail keeps coming, and since 2019 federal law guarantees back pay for workers who are furloughed or required to work without pay.

What actually shuts down

OPM explains that a shutdown furlough happens at the start of a fiscal year if no funds have been appropriated, or when a continuing resolution expires and no new one or full appropriations law is passed. An affected agency must shut down any activities funded by annual appropriations that are not excepted by law, and OPM notes agencies usually get very little lead time. Work that protects life and property is a common example of excepted work. Some agency functions are paid for in other ways, such as fees, and can keep going.

Social Security and SSI

In its October 1, 2025 shutdown notice, the Social Security Administration said payments to everyone receiving Social Security and SSI "will continue with no change in payment dates." Local offices stayed open with reduced services: people could still apply for benefits, appeal, change an address or direct deposit, and get a replacement card, but SSA said it could not provide proof of benefits letters or correct earnings records until the shutdown ended.

Mail

Mail delivery continues. The U.S. Postal Service says it generally receives no tax dollars for operating expenses and pays for its operations through the sale of postage, products and services, so it is not dependent on the annual funding bills that lapse.

Federal workers' pay

The Government Employee Fair Treatment Act of 2019, which became Public Law 116-1 on January 16, 2019, requires federal employees who are furloughed or required to work during a lapse in appropriations to be paid for that period, as early as possible after the lapse ends, regardless of scheduled pay dates, according to Congress.gov. Paychecks can still be delayed while the shutdown lasts.

Parks and other services

What stays open, from national parks to passport offices, depends on each agency's own shutdown plan. Check the agency's website before you travel or book an appointment.

The current deadline and what Congress is doing about it are on our government shutdown deadline 2026 page, with background on the Congress hub.

Quick answers

Do Social Security checks stop during a government shutdown?

No. SSA has said Social Security and SSI payments continue with no change in payment dates.

Does the mail run during a government shutdown?

Yes. USPS funds its operations through postage, products and services, not annual appropriations.

Do federal workers get back pay after a shutdown?

Yes. Public Law 116-1 (2019) requires pay for furloughed employees and those required to work during a lapse, after it ends.
