France protests: 550,000 march over prices and schools, 1,200 arrested

By The Trends Incoming team · Published 2026-10-18

Nonpartisan explainer: sourced facts, both sides represented, no endorsements.

About 550,000 people marched across France on Saturday, October 17, 2026, over the cost of living and conditions in schools, according to the French interior ministry. The CGT union estimated 800,000. Most marches were peaceful, but violence broke out in many cities, and Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said 1,200 people were arrested and 150 police officers were injured.

What happened

DateSaturday, Oct 17, 2026
TurnoutAbout 550,000 (interior ministry); 800,000 (CGT)
Arrests1,200
Police injured150
Biggest damageLimoges town hall largely destroyed by fire; Marseille and Lille city halls broken into

AFP and the BBC report looting and fires in cities including Paris, Marseille, Nice, Nantes, Bordeaux, Limoges, Lille, Toulouse and Strasbourg. The protests began last month at high schools in Paris' northern suburbs, over teacher shortages and run-down buildings, and grew to include trade unions and the Yellow Vest movement. AFP says inflation is the main grievance.

What each side says

The government: Lecornu said police faced "violence of extreme intensity" and that "no one will impose their political agenda through violence." President Emmanuel Macron said those who looted or attacked public buildings "will be prosecuted and punished."

The opposition: National Rally leader Marine Le Pen condemned the rioters and accused the far-left La France Insoumise of legitimising the violence. LFI leader Jean-Luc Mélenchon backed the marches, told protesters looting was "contrary to our methods," and accused police of going too far.

What comes next

France's next presidential election is in April and May 2027, and Macron can't run again, so the protests are already part of the campaign conversation. France's police watchdog is looking into complaints about police conduct.
