New York Minimum Wage 2027: No Raise on January 1

By The Trends Incoming team · Published Oct 19, 2026 · Last updated Oct 19, 2026

Plot twist: New York's minimum wage does not go up on January 1, 2027. It stays $17.00 in New York City, Long Island and Westchester and $16.00 in the rest of the state. The state labor department says one of the inflation "off-ramps" in state law was triggered, so the yearly inflation bump is skipped. Home care aides are the big exception.

🤔 Why no raise?

New York law lets the minimum wage rise with inflation each year, but it also has conditions, called off-ramps, that can stop the automatic increase. The state says one was met for 2027. Home care aide rates still rise because they're adjusted for inflation but capped at $3.00 above the standard minimum. Governor Hochul has proposed changing the off-ramp rules; nothing has changed yet.

Quick answers

Is New York's minimum wage going up in 2027?

No. It stays $17.00 in New York City, Long Island and Westchester and $16.00 in the rest of the state on January 1, 2027, per the New York State Department of Labor.

Why isn't New York's minimum wage increasing in 2027?

State law includes off-ramps that can block the automatic inflation increase, and the state says one of them was met for 2027.

Who gets a raise in New York in 2027?

Home care aides: $20.00 an hour downstate and $19.00 upstate from January 1, 2027.

What is the tipped minimum wage in New York for 2027?

For tipped food service workers, a $11.35 cash wage downstate and $10.70 upstate. For tipped service employees, $14.15 downstate and $13.30 upstate. Only hospitality employers can take a tip credit.
