2027 Standard Deduction: $16,600 Single, $33,200 Joint, $24,925 Head of Household

By The Trends Incoming team · Published Nov 20, 2026 · Last updated Nov 20, 2026

Short answer, checked Nov 20, 2026: for tax year 2027 the IRS standard deduction is $16,600 for single filers and for married filing separately, $33,200 for married filing jointly and surviving spouses, and $24,925 for head of household. The additional amount for age 65 or blindness is $1,700, or $2,100 if the person is unmarried and not a surviving spouse. Those figures are in Revenue Procedure 2026-35, section 3.15. News release IR-2026-133 (Nov 5, 2026) prints the three base amounts and says the joint figure is up $1,000 from 2026.

What the newsroom printed, and what it left in the PDF

IR-2026-133 leads with three numbers: $33,200 joint, $16,600 single and married filing separately, and $24,925 head of household. It does not repeat the age or blindness add-on, and it does not repeat the dependent cap. Those sit in section 3.15 of Rev. Proc. 2026-35, the same procedure the news release points to for "details about these annual adjustments." The 2027 tax brackets page keeps a one-row version of the add-ons next to the rate tables. The additional amount for age or blindness

Section 3.15(3) of Rev. Proc. 2026-35 says the additional standard deduction amount under Internal Revenue Code section 63(f), "for the aged or the blind," is $1,700 for 2027. It "is increased to $2,100 if the individual is also unmarried and not a surviving spouse." The 2026 procedure, Rev. Proc. 2025-32, used the same sentence with $1,650 and $2,050.

The procedure states that additional amount. It does not print a combined total for someone who is both 65 or older and blind. Section 63(f) is the code section the procedure cites for how the additional amount applies. This page does not add the figures together into a personal deduction.

Dependents

Section 3.15(2) caps the standard deduction of a person who may be claimed as a dependent by another taxpayer. For 2027 the cap is the greater of $1,400, or $500 plus that person's earned income. For 2026 it was the greater of $1,350, or $450 plus earned income. The same $1,400 figure shows up elsewhere in Rev. Proc. 2026-35 as the inflation-adjusted amount in section 63(c)(5)(A), which the procedure also uses in the kiddie-tax rules. That cross-reference is in section 3.02 of the 2027 procedure. It is a definition, not a suggestion about how a household should file.

Which return these numbers belong on

IR-2026-133 says the tax year 2027 adjustments generally apply to returns filed in 2028. The return most people file in spring 2027 is the 2026 return, and that return still uses the 2026 column above: $16,100, $32,200 and $24,150, from Rev. Proc. 2025-32.

The rate tables that sit on top of these deductions are on the 2027 brackets page, including the bracket calculator. Retirement caps for the same year are on the 401(k) and IRA limits page. The 2027 official numbers tracker lists every figure with the agency and the release date. Social Security's separate 2027 cost-of-living adjustment is on the COLA page.

Quick answers

What is the 2027 standard deduction for a single filer?

$16,600. Married people filing separate returns use the same $16,600. Both amounts are in IRS Revenue Procedure 2026-35, section 3.15, announced with news release IR-2026-133 on November 5, 2026.

What is the 2027 standard deduction for a married couple filing jointly?

$33,200, and a surviving spouse uses that same amount. IR-2026-133 says that is $1,000 more than 2026. Revenue Procedure 2025-32 set the 2026 joint amount at $32,200.

What is the 2027 head of household standard deduction?

$24,925. The 2026 amount in Revenue Procedure 2025-32 was $24,150. The difference between those two published figures is $775.

What is the extra standard deduction for age 65 or blindness in 2027?

Revenue Procedure 2026-35 sets the additional amount under section 63(f) for the aged or the blind at $1,700. The additional amount is $2,100 if the person is also unmarried and not a surviving spouse. For 2026 those amounts were $1,650 and $2,050.

What is the 2027 standard deduction cap for a dependent?

Someone who can be claimed as a dependent by another taxpayer cannot take more than the greater of $1,400, or $500 plus that person's earned income. For 2026 the cap was the greater of $1,350, or $450 plus earned income.

Do the 2027 amounts apply to the return filed in 2027?

No. IR-2026-133 says the tax year 2027 adjustments generally apply to returns filed in 2028. A return filed in 2027 is generally a 2026 return and uses the 2026 standard deduction.
