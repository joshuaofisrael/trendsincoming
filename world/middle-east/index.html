The quick version

This weekend: Houthi missile and drone attacks on Saudi Arabia. The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed two ballistic missiles fired toward Taif and Yanbu on Saturday, October 24, and Saudi authorities said two people were killed in Dammam, CNBC and Reuters report. The Houthis claimed further strikes on airports and oil sites that Saudi Arabia has not confirmed. The big question this week is the Strait of Hormuz. Iran says it would reopen the strait under a seven-day plan if the U.S. meets its conditions, including ending hostilities, lifting the naval blockade and easing oil sanctions. Washington hasn't accepted, and Qatar is mediating. Meanwhile, Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia keep escalating.

Saudi Arabia and the Houthis: the latest

Civil defense alerts sounded in Taif, Yanbu, Mecca and Jeddah on Saturday before clearing about 10 minutes later, per CNBC and Reuters. Reuters sources said Aramco told staff to evacuate its Dhahran headquarters. The Houthis said they targeted King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, Aramco facilities in Yanbu, the Rabigh refinery and the East-West pipeline, Anadolu reports. Those are the group's claims and have not been confirmed by Saudi authorities. In Jordan, a deadly attack on a church funeral in Karak has killed 9.

U.S. President Donald Trump said a strike on Iran was being considered, "maybe tomorrow," and also said a deal was possible, Anadolu reports. Israel holds Knesset elections on Tuesday, October 27.

Why a strip of water matters to your wallet

A large share of the world's oil and gas passes through Hormuz, so every attack or blockade headline moves prices. When traders think a reopening is closer, oil eases, like it did Monday morning. When talks stall, it jumps. That's why our gas prices page watches this region closely.
