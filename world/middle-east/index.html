Hormuz: more ships hit, Brent tops $112

The quick version

The big question this week is the Strait of Hormuz. Iran says it would reopen the strait under a seven-day plan if the U.S. meets its conditions, including ending hostilities, lifting the naval blockade and easing oil sanctions. Washington hasn't accepted, and Qatar is mediating. Meanwhile, Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia keep escalating.

Why a strip of water matters to your wallet

A large share of the world's oil and gas passes through Hormuz, so every attack or blockade headline moves prices. When traders think a reopening is closer, oil eases, like it did Monday morning. When talks stall, it jumps. That's why our gas prices page watches this region closely.

Quick answers

Is the Strait of Hormuz open?

Shipping has faced repeated attacks, and Iran has offered a seven-day plan to reopen it if the U.S. meets its conditions. As of October 19, 2026, no deal has been announced.

Why are oil prices so high?

Attacks near Hormuz and on Russian refineries have squeezed supply. Brent crude touched $110 on October 16, 2026.

Are flights to Saudi Arabia suspended?

Several airlines suspended flights after strikes on Riyadh's airport. Check with your airline.
