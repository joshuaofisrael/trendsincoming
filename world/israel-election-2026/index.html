The quick version

Israelis are voting today, Tuesday, October 27. Polls are open from 7 a.m. to 10 p.m. Israel time (05:00 to 20:00 GMT, 1 a.m. to 4 p.m. ET), with TV exit polls as soon as they close. There are 7,331,981 eligible voters, 12,018 polling stations and 36 party lists, Ynet reports. Official results are due by November 4. Israel votes on Tuesday, October 27, 2026, for all 120 seats in the Knesset. A coalition needs 61. The final KAN and Channel 12 polls put Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc at 50 seats and the opposition at 52, Anadolu reports, while the final Times of Israel (Zman Yisrael) poll showed the two camps tied at 51-51. Neither side reaches a majority in those polls, so coalition talks after the vote are likely to decide the next government.

Election day, the basics

Exit polls are projections, not results, and the count of double-envelope ballots (soldiers, diplomats and others) can shift seats in the days after. It's the first national election since October 7, 2023, the Jerusalem Post notes. Small parties near the 3.25% threshold matter a lot: the Taub Center estimates about 165,635 votes are needed to cross it at 70% turnout, per the Post.

What the final polls say

The last surveys allowed before the legal blackout put the race very close. Anadolu reports the KAN and Channel 12 polls with Yashar on 22 seats, Likud on 20 and Naftali Bennett's Together on 14, and the opposition bloc two seats ahead of Netanyahu's camp. The Times of Israel's final poll had the blocs dead even. Polls carry margins of error and small parties near the 3.25% threshold can swing the math.

What happens after the vote

The president asks the candidate with the best chance of forming a majority to try to build a coalition. If no one can, the Times of Israel notes, Israel could head to another election in early 2027, with Netanyahu remaining as caretaker prime minister in the meantime.

How many seats are needed to form a government in Israel?

What do the final Israeli election polls show?

The final KAN and Channel 12 polls showed Netanyahu's bloc at 50 seats and the opposition at 52, per Anadolu. The final Times of Israel poll showed a 51-51 tie.

Why are there no new Israeli polls before the vote?

Israeli law bans publishing polls from the Friday before election day until polls close.
