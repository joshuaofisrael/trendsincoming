The quick version

Protests over the cost of living and conditions in schools drew about 550,000 people across France on Saturday, October 17, 2026, per the interior ministry. Violence and looting over the weekend pushed arrests to about 2,400, including 351 in Paris, per live coverage on October 19. President Macron called for calm and refused Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez's resignation, and Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu is holding a crisis meeting.

Why people are protesting

It began with high school students angry about teacher shortages and run-down buildings, then grew into a wider movement about prices, fuel costs, inequality and public services. Trade unions joined, and the Yellow Vest movement, famous for its 2018 fuel-tax protests, called Saturday's "yellow and youth" marches. AFP reports that inflation, pushed up in part by the war in the Middle East, is the main grievance. On October 8 the education minister promised 3,000 extra supply teachers, but the protests kept growing.

What each side is saying

  • The government: Lecornu said police faced "violence of extreme intensity" and that "no one will impose their political agenda through violence." President Macron said those who looted or attacked public buildings "will be prosecuted and punished."
  • The far right: National Rally leader Marine Le Pen condemned the rioters and accused the far-left party La France Insoumise of legitimising the violence.
  • The far left: LFI leader Jean-Luc Mélenchon supported the marches, told demonstrators to stay away from looting, calling it "contrary to our methods," and accused police of going too far.
  • Protesters and police unions: many marchers blamed a violent fringe and criticized police tactics; the police watchdog is investigating at least two dozen complaints. A police union said officers are "at breaking point."

Why are people protesting in France in October 2026?

How many people protested in France on October 17, 2026?

How many people were arrested in the France protests?
