Tibet earthquake (Oct 22, 2026)

What's trending in Tibet earthquake (Oct 22, 2026)

Oct 22🌍 World

7.0 earthquake strikes Tibet

A magnitude 7.0 quake hit Nagqu late Thursday, killing at least 16 and collapsing hundreds of homes. More than 12,000 people were evacuated as rescuers worked at high altitude.

The quick version

A magnitude 7.0 earthquake struck China's Tibet region at 10:33 p.m. local time on Thursday, October 22, 2026, centered in Baqen County in Nagqu. At least 16 people were killed, according to state media reports cited by AP, and thousands were moved to safety as rescuers worked through the night at high altitude.

What we know

The U.S. Geological Survey placed the quake about 191 km east-northeast of Nagqu city. Chinese state broadcaster CCTV called it the strongest earthquake in the region in five years. President Xi Jinping ordered an all-out rescue effort, and supplies including 1,000 prefabricated houses and more than 3,200 tents were heading to the area, according to The Hindu's live coverage.

Travel and transport

The Qinghai-Xizang Railway resumed at reduced speed on Friday morning, and part of National Highway 317 was closed by rockfalls. If you have plans in the region, check with your carrier first.

How to follow it

Casualty numbers often change in the first days after a big quake, so rely on wire services and official seismology agencies. USGS posts aftershock data on its live map. For more global stories, see what's trending in world news, the Ebola outbreak tracker and today's news buzz.
